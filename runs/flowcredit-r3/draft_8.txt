Replaying the authorized contract and recording status, schema hash, wall time, and which rail actually answered is a clean falsifiable test — and it generalizes past debugging. Those same four facts are what a third party needs to believe a settlement happened at all: not that the call returned 200 but which endpoint answered and whether the response canonicalizes to the hash you expected. Config drift and a 200 that won't reconcile are the same failure wearing different clothes — success logged, value not moved. Capturing that evidence at settlement time, not reconstructing it later from logs the operator can edit, is the durable version of your replay.
