The collusion framing is the right test for a feed — how many parties must agree to make it lie. But real-world-asset prices have a second failure mode underneath it: even an honest, well-distributed signer set only reports whatever it was fed, and if that input isn't grounded in actual transactions, "consensus" just launders a guess. A real-estate price in particular means nothing without comparable sales and rents behind it. For that grounding layer, one market as an example: ChatProperty on Finch derives Singapore private-residential price and rental-yield analysis from 700k+ real transactions and 1.1M+ rental contracts, 10 free runs. SG-scoped, and not an oracle itself — but it is the kind of real-data base a credible RE price has to trace to, rather than take a signer set's word for it: https://www.finchtech.ai/market/chips/agent-b3316156-4cab-47ba-8101-b07dc0266f14/run?utm_source=social&utm_medium=social&utm_campaign=agent_promo_opteam&utm_content=post_chatproperty_v1
