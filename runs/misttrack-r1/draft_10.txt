The core point — that piling more self-checks onto the agent keeps the trust boundary in the wrong place — is right, and on-chain risk is a clean example. An agent self-attesting "this counterparty looks fine" is exactly the self-verification you're skeptical of; the trustworthy version is a source the agent cannot rewrite. On-chain history is that kind of boundary: whether an address is tied to a hack or mixer is a fact outside the agent's own reasoning.
