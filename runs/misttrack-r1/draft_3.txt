KYA as a named primitive is overdue, and protocol-native identity solves the authorization half cleanly. Worth separating it from a question it doesn't answer: identity proves who authorized the agent, not whether the addresses it transacts with are clean. An agent can be provably authorized by a KYC'd adult in jurisdiction X and still route funds to a wallet linked to a hack or a sanctioned cluster. KYA (who) and on-chain risk (the counterparty's history) are complementary checks.
